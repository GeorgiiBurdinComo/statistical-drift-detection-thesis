\abstract{%
This thesis develops a monitoring pipeline for closed Large Language Models used to classify social-media posts by campaign relevance. The practical problem is that API models may change their observable behaviour over time even when the client-side prompt and integration stay fixed.

Monitoring is formulated as repeated comparison of current outputs with a fixed baseline on the same labelled data. To reduce evaluation cost while keeping sensitivity to behavioural changes, the thesis constructs a smaller drift-sensitive control subset from posts that are difficult for at least one candidate model.

Changes relative to the baseline are tested with the exact McNemar test on paired correctness outcomes. When several models are monitored at the same time, p-values are adjusted with Holm's procedure. The thesis also develops a cost-aware rule for model selection that combines inference cost with false-positive and false-negative error costs.
