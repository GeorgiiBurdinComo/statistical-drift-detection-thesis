\chapter{Experimental Results}
\label{chap:experimental-results}

This chapter evaluates the complete operational chain studied in the main thesis: repeated panel monitoring, exact paired testing with multiplicity control, and cost-aware candidate ranking after escalation. The evidence window is 15~March--6~July~2026.

\section{Monitoring results}
\label{sec:drift-results}

The canonical monitoring set contains 187 eligible scheduled panel runs from 12 models. A model's first eligible run is its baseline; all later eligible runs for that model yield 173 baseline-to-later paired comparisons. Missing outputs are removed pairwise, so the effective sample size can be below the nominal panel size of 300.

At the one-sided nominal level \(\alpha=.05\), three comparisons had \(p_{\mathrm{exact}}<.05\). Applying Holm correction globally across all 173 comparisons left one rejection. That rejection also exceeded the declared operational degradation floor of 3 percentage points, and was therefore the only operational alert. The distinction is important: a small unadjusted \(p_{\mathrm{exact}}\)-value alone did not constitute an alert.

\paragraph{The operational alert.}
The alert compared \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} on 15~March with its 22~March run. There were \(n_{\mathrm{paired}}=296\) complete pairs: \(b=49\) items degraded from correct to incorrect and \(c=12\) improved. Accuracy fell from \(0.7872\) to \(0.6622\), a change of \(-12.5\) percentage points. The one-sided exact McNemar p-value was \(p_{\mathrm{exact}}=9.849\times10^{-7}\); the global Holm-adjusted value was \(p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1.704\times10^{-4}\). Thus both the statistical and operational conditions were met. The paired evidence identifies a change in this model's observed correctness on the panel. Provider state and other causal mechanisms remain unobserved.

\section{Realised discordance and detectable effects}
\label{sec:realized-discordance}

Across the 173 comparisons, median observed discordance was \(0.02685\), where discordance is \((b+c)/n_{\mathrm{paired}}\). This historical value describes how much paired movement occurred in these runs. Future failures may generate much larger discordance, as the alert itself did (\(61/296=0.2061\)); low historical discordance therefore says little about a model's intrinsic stability or the magnitude of future changes.

The historical size calculation used pilot discordance \(\rho_{\mathrm{pilot}}=137/1199=0.1143\), a target degradation of 5 percentage points, and nominal one-sided \(\alpha=.05\). The construction-time approximation suggested \(n_{\mathrm{planned}}=273\), which was rounded upward to \(n_{\mathrm{panel}}=300\). Under the exact unconditional calculation, that rounding raises power from \(0.756\) to \(0.795\); \(n_{\mathrm{planned}}=304\), rather than 273, is the smallest size reaching \(0.80\) under the stated assumptions. The implemented panel therefore came close to its intended nominal sensitivity.

Exact unconditional power calculations also clarify what a 300-item panel can establish outside that single sizing point. At the nearby design discordance \(\rho_{\mathrm{design}}=.12\), the 80\%-power minimum detectable degradation is \(5.15\) percentage points at nominal \(\alpha=.05\), but \(8.32\) percentage points at the family-conservative level \(\alpha=.05/173\). For a 3-percentage-point degradation, exact power is only \(0.387\) nominally and \(0.017\) under the family-conservative level.

The recorded API charges provide a monetary comparison in addition to the item-count reduction. For the eight models with both measurements, the 22~May full-benchmark sweep cost \(\$43.10\). The mean panel sweep over 18 and 25~May cost \(\$12.26\), a reduction of \(\$30.84\), or \(71.6\%\). The ratio is \(3.51\), slightly below the \(1199/300=4.00\) item ratio because request lengths, outputs, and model prices differ.

For the three production models---\texttt{gpt-5-mini}, \texttt{gpt-5-nano}, and \texttt{gpt-5}---one panel sweep cost \(\$2.74\), compared with \(\$9.53\) for a full-benchmark sweep. Weekly panel monitoring therefore costs approximately \(\$142.42\) per year in API charges.

The error cost of an undetected episode can be estimated conditionally on the penalties used in Section~\ref{sec:cost-aware-comparison}. On the 296 items shared by the \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} baseline and alert runs, the confusion counts changed from \((\mathrm{FP},\mathrm{FN})=(2,61)\) to \((0,100)\). Holding inference price fixed, the incremental error loss per affected classification is
\[
    \Delta L_{\mathrm{err}}
    =\frac{0.155(0-2)+0.031(100-61)}{296}
    =\$0.003037.
\]

The first three rows represent separate failure scenarios in which exactly one production model develops an error shift of the observed severity. If \texttt{gpt-5-nano} degrades, its own weekly panel cost breaks even after approximately 37 affected posts; the corresponding thresholds are 142 posts for \texttt{gpt-5-mini} and 723 for \texttt{gpt-5}. If monitoring is treated as one indivisible portfolio covering all three models, the relevant weekly cost is \(\$2.74\) and the break-even volume is 902 affected classifications, regardless of which single model fails. The annual portfolio cost of \(\$142.42\) corresponds to approximately \(46{,}891\) affected classifications.

These calculations are scenarios rather than universal incident costs: another degradation may change FP and FN counts differently, and simultaneous failures would require adding their affected volumes. They nevertheless show why all three models should remain monitored. Each model is a separate failure point, while the total weekly insurance cost remains lower than the estimated loss from one similarly severe undetected episode once approximately 902 posts pass through the failed model.

This qualifies RQ2. The panel demonstrates a substantial measured reduction in API expenditure while retaining sensitivity to large changes such as the observed episode. Useful sensitivity still depends jointly on effect size, discordance, and multiplicity control. Disagreement determined which items entered the panel, whereas the pilot power calculation determined how many were retained. Its relative statistical efficiency and representativeness remain open because no equal-size random-panel comparison was run.

\section{Cost-aware comparison}
\label{sec:cost-aware-comparison}

The deployment comparison uses the 22~May common complete-case intersection: 1113 items observed for all eight candidate models. This avoids changing the evaluated item set across candidates. The illustrative operating point is
\[
    w=\frac{C_{\mathrm{FN}}}{C_{\mathrm{FP}}}=0.2,
    \qquad
    \lambda=C_{\mathrm{FP}}+C_{\mathrm{FN}}=0.186,
\]
equivalently \(C_{\mathrm{FP}}=0.155\) and \(C_{\mathrm{FN}}=0.031\) USD, in addition to mean inference cost. Thus a false positive is assigned five times the cost of a false negative, reflecting the operational burden of sending irrelevant posts into downstream processing.

\texttt{gpt-5-mini} minimises plug-in loss at \(0.003697\). It balances low inference cost with five false positives and 62 false negatives. The cheaper \texttt{gpt-5-nano} is a close second at \(0.003779\): saving on inference and one false positive does not offset its 104 false negatives. The accuracy leader \texttt{gpt-5.2} reaches \(0.97844\) accuracy but has loss \(0.01098\), mainly because its inference cost and 17 false positives are expensive under the stated business asymmetry. Therefore neither maximum accuracy nor minimum API price alone selects the preferred deployment model.

The penalties encode the intended business preference, but they are not estimated from realised downstream losses. The choice is also close: the loss advantage of \texttt{gpt-5-mini} over \texttt{gpt-5-nano} is about \(8.2\times10^{-5}\) USD per request. The decision map therefore matters as much as the point ranking: a modest change in the assumed penalties can change the winner. Sampling uncertainty in the plug-in ranking is not quantified here.

\section{What these results establish}
\label{sec:what-established}

The results show that the monitoring procedure performed its intended task. Across 173 scheduled comparisons, it detected the one severe episode: a \(12.5\)-percentage-point decrease with 49 regressions against 12 improvements, significant after global Holm correction and above the pre-declared magnitude floor. None of the other 172 comparisons met both alert conditions. This does not prove that their underlying effects were exactly zero, but it does show that none supplied corrected evidence of a degradation large enough to trigger operational escalation. The procedure therefore separated a material change from ordinary week-to-week variation without relying on unadjusted significance alone.

The exact provider-side cause of the alert is not identifiable from a black-box API. Model weights, routing, serving configuration, and execution state are not exposed by the provider, so no external evaluation can determine which internal mechanism changed. What the observations can establish is narrower and operationally sufficient: the failure was model-specific, was not accompanied by a comparable same-period decline in the other monitored models, and disappeared in subsequent runs without a local prompt, parser, dataset, or pipeline change. These controls make a shared failure in the evaluation pipeline unlikely; they do not justify inventing an unavailable provider-internal explanation.

The research questions are answered at the level supported by the experiment. For RQ2, the reduced panel lowered the observed sweep cost by \(71.6\%\) and retained sufficient sensitivity to detect the observed large degradation; the power analysis states explicitly which smaller effects cannot be guaranteed under multiplicity control. For RQ3, an alert requires the pre-specified conjunction of directional exact McNemar evidence, family-wise Holm control, and the three-percentage-point operational floor, and the experiment demonstrates that rule end to end. For RQ4, ranking by the declared empirical loss selects \texttt{gpt-5-mini} rather than the most accurate or the cheapest model, while the decision map shows how that choice changes with the cost assumptions.

Taken together, the results validate the complete detection-and-selection pipeline: material degradation was detected, non-alerting variation was not over-interpreted, and the alert was translated into an explicit cost-aware deployment decision. Causal diagnosis inside a closed provider model is outside the observable scope of this pipeline, not a missing statistical conclusion.
