\chapter{Experimental Results}
\label{chap:experimental-results}

This chapter reports the recorded monitoring window from 15~March to 6~July~2026 and the later common-case candidate comparison.

\section{Monitoring results}
\label{sec:drift-results}

The timeline contains 187 eligible runs. Twelve recurring model labels contribute 185 runs and 173 baseline-to-later paired comparisons. Missing outputs are removed pairwise, so the effective sample size can be below 300.

The two singleton labels, \texttt{gemini-2.0-flash} and \texttt{gpt-5.4-2026-03-05}, each appear once. A model's first eligible run is its baseline, so these two runs remain recorded baselines until a later comparison exists. The frozen labelled sets make that admission routine: a newly released model is scored on the same posts as the existing candidates, compared immediately with contemporaneous series, and then paired against its own first-run baseline as soon as later runs exist.

At the one-sided nominal level \(\alpha=.05\), three comparisons produced monitoring signals with \(p_{\mathrm{exact}}<.05\). These signals opened review on the fixed panel; deployment significance was determined separately.

\paragraph{The March signal and rebound.}
The strongest signal compared \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} on 15~March with its 22~March run. There were \(n_{\mathrm{paired}}=296\) complete pairs: \(b=49\) items changed from correct to incorrect and \(c=12\) changed from incorrect to correct. Accuracy fell from \(0.7872\) to \(0.6622\), a change of \(-12.5\) percentage points. The one-sided exact p-value was \(9.849\times10^{-7}\).

The next run, 22--28~March, reversed the movement: on 297 complete pairs accuracy rose from \(0.6633\) to \(0.7946\), with 11 regressions and 50 improvements. From 15 to 28~March the net change was \(+0.67\) percentage points, so the episode did not persist. Same-period models had no comparable drop, which weakens a shared local-failure explanation. \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} was a monitored candidate; no production deployment was changed.

\section{Realised discordance and detectable effects}
\label{sec:realized-discordance}

Across the 173 comparisons, median observed discordance was \(0.02685\), where discordance is \((b+c)/n_{\mathrm{paired}}\). This historical value describes how much paired movement occurred in these runs. Future failures may generate much larger discordance, as the March signal itself did (\(61/296=0.2061\)); low historical discordance therefore says little about a model's intrinsic stability or the magnitude of future changes.

The historical size calculation used pilot discordance \(\rho_{\mathrm{pilot}}=137/1199=0.1143\), a target degradation of 5 percentage points, and nominal one-sided \(\alpha=.05\). The construction-time approximation suggested \(n_{\mathrm{planned}}=273\), which was rounded upward to \(n_{\mathrm{panel}}=300\). At this implemented size, exact unconditional power rounds to \(0.80\).

Exact unconditional power calculations also clarify what a 300-item panel can establish outside that single sizing point. At the nearby design discordance \(\rho_{\mathrm{design}}=.12\), the 80\%-power minimum detectable degradation is \(5.15\) percentage points at nominal \(\alpha=.05\), but \(8.32\) percentage points at the family-conservative level \(\alpha=.05/173\). For a 5-percentage-point degradation, exact power is \(0.776\) nominally and \(0.137\) under the family-conservative level; for an 8-percentage-point degradation, it rises to \(0.994\) and \(0.736\), respectively. On this panel, degradations of 5--8 percentage points provide a practical orientation band: they matter operationally, yet strict multiplicity control still makes them hard to detect reliably.

The error cost of an undetected episode can be estimated conditionally on the penalties used in Section~\ref{sec:cost-aware-comparison}. On the 296 items shared by the \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} baseline and signal runs, the confusion counts changed from \((\mathrm{FP},\mathrm{FN})=(2,61)\) to \((0,100)\). Holding inference price fixed, the resulting incremental error loss per affected classification is
\[
    \Delta L_{\mathrm{err}}
    =\frac{0.155(0-2)+0.031(100-61)}{296}
    =\$0.003037.
\]

\section{Cost-aware comparison}
\label{sec:cost-aware-comparison}

The deployment comparison uses the 22~May common complete-case intersection: 1112 items observed for all eight candidate models. This avoids changing the evaluated item set across candidates. The illustrative operating point is
\[
    w=\frac{C_{\mathrm{FN}}}{C_{\mathrm{FP}}}=0.2,
    \qquad
    \lambda=C_{\mathrm{FP}}+C_{\mathrm{FN}}=0.186,
\]
which is equivalent to \(C_{\mathrm{FP}}=0.155\) and \(C_{\mathrm{FN}}=0.031\) USD, in addition to mean inference cost. At this operating point, a false positive is assigned five times the cost of a false negative. This reflects the operational burden of sending irrelevant posts into downstream processing.

\section{Scope of the result}
\label{sec:what-established}

Across 173 comparisons, three nominal signals were reviewed. The March \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} episode recovered in the next run, and the two \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5} signals were not acted on because that model was not deployed. A retrospective closed-family review across the 173 comparisons leaves only the \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} episode after global Holm correction. For the three-model production portfolio, the panel reduced measured sweep cost by \(71.2\%\). The May complete-case comparison selected \texttt{gpt-5-mini} only under its declared loss scenario. None of these observations proves a provider-side model update, a production-risk shift, or universal superiority of the panel sampler.
