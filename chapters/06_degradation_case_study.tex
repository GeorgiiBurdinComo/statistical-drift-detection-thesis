\section{Transient versus sustained degradation}
\label{sec:transient-provider-degradation}

A statistically significant accuracy drop on $\mathcal{D}_n$ does not necessarily indicate a permanent change in model behaviour. Between 22 and 23~March~2026, \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} exhibited subset accuracy below that of \texttt{gpt-4.1-nano}, yet returned to its prior level within two days without any intervention. Because the affected model was not in active deployment at the time, no corrective action was required.

This episode motivates a distinction between \emph{transient} and \emph{sustained} degradation. A transient drop may resolve autonomously, whereas sustained degradation warrants either a model switch or prompt optimisation (Chapter~\ref{ch:prompt-optimisation}). In the monitoring protocol, the recommended response to an initial alert is therefore to switch to a fallback model and continue observing; prompt optimisation is triggered only if the regression persists across multiple evaluation windows.
