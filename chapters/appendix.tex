\appendix
\chapter{Conditional Distribution of the McNemar Statistic}
\label{app:mcnemar-derivation}

This appendix derives the conditional law used for the exact test in Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section} and for the sample-size calculation in Section~\ref{sec:monitoring-sample-size}.

For item $i$ the paired correctness outcome $(E_{i,t_1},E_{i,t_2})$ falls into one of four categories, with probabilities
\[
p_a=P(1,1),\quad
p_b=P(1,0),\quad
p_c=P(0,1),\quad
p_d=P(0,0),
\qquad
p_a+p_b+p_c+p_d=1.
\]
Assume the $n$ paired items are independent and identically distributed across these categories. Then the counts are multinomial,
\begin{equation}
    (a,b,c,d)\sim \mathrm{Multinomial}(n;p_a,p_b,p_c,p_d).
\end{equation}

McNemar's test uses only the discordant counts. Write $m=b+c$ and fix an integer $k\in\{0,\dots,m\}$. From the multinomial model,
\begin{equation}
    P(b=k,\;c=m-k)
    =
    \frac{n!}{k!\,(m-k)!\,(n-m)!}
    (p_a+p_d)^{n-m}p_b^k p_c^{m-k},
\end{equation}
and marginalising over the split of the $m$ discordant items,
\begin{equation}
    P(b+c=m)
    =
    \frac{n!}{m!\,(n-m)!}
    (p_a+p_d)^{n-m}(p_b+p_c)^m.
\end{equation}
Dividing, the factors $(p_a+p_d)^{n-m}$, $n!$ and $(n-m)!$ all cancel:
\begin{align}
P(b=k\mid b+c=m)
&=
\frac{
\frac{n!}{k!\,(m-k)!\,(n-m)!}
(p_a+p_d)^{n-m}p_b^k p_c^{m-k}
}{
\frac{n!}{m!\,(n-m)!}
(p_a+p_d)^{n-m}(p_b+p_c)^m
} \\
&=
\binom{m}{k}
\left(\frac{p_b}{p_b+p_c}\right)^k
\left(\frac{p_c}{p_b+p_c}\right)^{m-k}.
\end{align}
This is a binomial mass function, so
\begin{equation}
    b\mid (b+c=m)\sim \mathrm{Binomial}(m,\eta),
    \qquad
    \eta=\frac{p_b}{p_b+p_c}=\frac{p_b}{\rho}.
    \label{eq:app-conditional-binomial}
\end{equation}
The concordant cells $a$ and $d$ have dropped out entirely: conditioning on $m$ removes all dependence on how many items were stable.

Writing $\rho=p_b+p_c$ and $\delta=p_b-p_c$ gives $p_b=(\rho+\delta)/2$ and $p_c=(\rho-\delta)/2$, hence
\begin{equation}
    \eta=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2\rho}.
    \label{eq:app-eta}
\end{equation}
So $\eta$ depends on the directional imbalance only through the ratio $\delta/\rho$. Under the null hypothesis of no directional change, $H_0:p_b=p_c$, the imbalance $\delta$ is zero and therefore
\begin{equation}
    b\mid (b+c=m)\sim \mathrm{Binomial}(m,1/2):
\end{equation}
once the number of discordant items is fixed, each is equally likely to be a regression or an improvement. This is the basis of the exact conditional test. For one-sided degradation detection the alternative is $H_a:\eta>1/2$.
