\chapter{Cost-Aware Deployment Decisions}
\label{ch:cost-deployment-utility}

\noindent Chapter~\ref{ch:drift} establishes a directional monitoring signal on the fixed panel \(\mathcal{D}_n\). When that signal and its diagnostics support escalation, the next step is a broader rerun on the full benchmark \(\mathcal{D}_N\) of 1199 labelled posts. That rerun serves a different role from the sentinel panel: it places candidate model--prompt configurations on a common evaluation basis and supplies the false-positive counts, false-negative counts, and observed inference charges needed for deployment comparison. This chapter turns those benchmark outcomes into a model-selection rule under declared task-specific losses.

\section{From panel signal to benchmark comparison}

Let
\(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cand}}=\{m_1,\ldots,m_J\}\) be the candidate set of model--prompt configurations at evaluation time \(t\).

Let \(\mathcal{B}_t\subseteq\mathcal{D}_N\) be the benchmark items with a valid parsed prediction and usage record for every candidate, and write \(n_t=|\mathcal{B}_t|>0\). Using the same items for every candidate keeps the ranking attributable to model differences rather than to item composition.

\paragraph{Step 1: confusion-matrix decomposition.}
On the common rerun set \(\mathcal{B}_t\), candidate \(m\) at evaluation time \(t\) produces confusion counts \(\mathrm{TP}_t(m)\), \(\mathrm{FP}_t(m)\), \(\mathrm{TN}_t(m)\), and \(\mathrm{FN}_t(m)\) satisfying
\[
    \mathrm{TP}_t(m)
    +\mathrm{FP}_t(m)
    +\mathrm{TN}_t(m)
    +\mathrm{FN}_t(m)
    =
    n_t.
\]
Let \(c_t(m)\) denote the mean inference charge in USD per request on \(\mathcal{B}_t\). This quantity can differ across models, so the comparison uses the average observed inference cost for candidate \(m\) over the requests in \(\mathcal{B}_t\). The scenario penalties \(C_{\mathrm{FP}}\ge0\) and \(C_{\mathrm{FN}}\ge0\) assign a business cost in USD to one false positive and one false negative, respectively.

\paragraph{Step 2: total batch cost.}
The total batch cost is the sum of inference cost and business-error cost:
\begin{description}
    \item[\(n_t\)] size of the common rerun set;
    \item[\(c_t(m)\)] mean inference charge for candidate \(m\);
    \item[\(\mathrm{FP}_t(m),\ \mathrm{FN}_t(m)\)] observed false-positive and false-negative counts;
    \item[\(C_{\mathrm{FP}},\ C_{\mathrm{FN}}\)] assigned costs of one false positive and one false negative.
\end{description}
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{TotalCost}_t(m)
    =
    \underbrace{n_t c_t(m)}_{\text{inference}}
    +
    \underbrace{
        C_{\mathrm{FP}}\mathrm{FP}_t(m)
        +C_{\mathrm{FN}}\mathrm{FN}_t(m)
    }_{\text{business error cost}}.
    \label{eq:total-batch-cost}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 3: per-request expected cost.}
Dividing by \(n_t\) gives the empirical per-request loss
\begin{equation}
L_t(m)
=
c_t(m)
+
\frac{
    C_{\mathrm{FP}}\mathrm{FP}_t(m)
    +C_{\mathrm{FN}}\mathrm{FN}_t(m)
}{n_t}.
\label{eq:expected-cost}
\end{equation}
The counts and charges are observed on \(\mathcal{B}_t\), whereas \(C_{\mathrm{FP}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{FN}}\) are declared scenario inputs. Changing the penalties therefore changes the deployment objective.

\paragraph{Step 4: model selection rule under fixed costs.}
When \(C_{\mathrm{FP}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{FN}}\) are declared, the preferred deployment candidate is
\begin{equation}
    m_t^\star
    \in
    \arg\min_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cand}}}
    L_t(m).
    \label{eq:model-selection}
\end{equation}
The set-valued \(\arg\min\) allows ties and therefore leaves room for a declared secondary rule when two candidates have the same loss. Table~\ref{tab:chapter4-cost-summary} illustrates the rule at one declared setting, \(C_{\mathrm{FP}}=0.155\) USD and \(C_{\mathrm{FN}}=0.031\) USD.

At this operating point, \texttt{gpt-5-mini} minimises the loss at \(0.0037\) USD per request. It combines five false positives and 62 false negatives with a low mean inference charge. \section{Preference parameters and decision map}
\label{sec:cost-parameters-uncertainty}

Two parameters separate the relative error trade-off from the overall severity of business errors:
\begin{equation}
    w
    =
    \frac{C_{\mathrm{FN}}}{C_{\mathrm{FP}}},
    \qquad
    \lambda
    =
    C_{\mathrm{FP}}+C_{\mathrm{FN}}.
    \label{eq:R-lambda-definition}
\end{equation}
Here \(w\) measures how costly a false negative is relative to a false positive, while \(\lambda\) measures how much business errors matter relative to inference price. The original penalties can be recovered from
\[
    C_{\mathrm{FP}}=\frac{\lambda}{1+w},
    \qquad
    C_{\mathrm{FN}}=\frac{\lambda w}{1+w}.
\]
Substituting these expressions into Equation~\eqref{eq:expected-cost} gives
\begin{align}
L_t(m;w,\lambda)
&=
c_t(m)
+
\frac{\lambda}{1+w}
\frac{
    \mathrm{FP}_t(m)+w\mathrm{FN}_t(m)
}{n_t}.
\label{eq:expected-cost-lambda-R}
\end{align}
This form exposes the decision logic directly: increasing \(\lambda\) gives more weight to business errors relative to API cost, and increasing \(w\) gives more weight to false negatives relative to false positives.

A deployment request becomes operational once \(w\) and \(\lambda\) are declared. Substituting that pair into Equation~\eqref{eq:expected-cost-lambda-R} yields one loss value for each candidate on the common rerun set, and the minimum of those values determines the preferred deployment model. Table~\ref{tab:chapter4-cost-summary} shows one illustrative operating point.

\section{From business requirements to model choice}
\label{sec:constraint-based-selection}

In practice, a deployment request starts with two questions.\\
First, how costly should one business error be relative to one model call? This fixes \(\lambda\).\\
Second, which error matters more, a false positive or a false negative? This fixes \(w\). Once those two inputs are declared, Equation~\eqref{eq:expected-cost-lambda-R} and Table~\ref{tab:chapter4-cost-summary} identify the preferred candidate.

The same comparison is available interactively in the expected-cost dashboard view of Section~\ref{sec:metrics-dashboard}: a reviewer enters \(C_{\mathrm{FP}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{FN}}\) (or their ratio), and the ranking highlights the current minimum-loss model under those scenario prices (Figure~\ref{fig:dashboard-expected-cost}).

\paragraph{Worked comparison: \texttt{gpt-5-mini} versus \texttt{gpt-5-nano}.}
On the shared rerun set, the loss formula is
\[
    L_t(m)
    =
    c_t(m)
    +
    \frac{
        C_{\mathrm{FP}}\mathrm{FP}_t(m)
        +C_{\mathrm{FN}}\mathrm{FN}_t(m)
    }{n_t},
\]
with \(n_t=1112\), \(C_{\mathrm{FP}}=0.155\), and \(C_{\mathrm{FN}}=0.031\). Substituting the observed values for both models gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{\texttt{mini}}
&=
0.0012740
+
\frac{0.155\times5+0.031\times62}{1112}\\
&=
0.0012740
+
0.0006969
+
0.0017284\\
&=
0.0036994,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
L_{\texttt{nano}}
&=
0.0003258
+
\frac{0.155\times5+0.031\times105}{1112}\\
&=
0.0003258
+
0.0006969
+
0.0029272\\
&=
0.0039499.
\end{aligned}
\]
The same comparison can be written in the \((w,\lambda)\) parametrization with \(w=0.2\) and \(\lambda=0.186\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{\texttt{mini}}
&=
0.0012740
+
\frac{0.186}{1+0.2}
\frac{5+0.2\times62}{1112}\\
&=
0.0012740
+
\frac{0.186}{1.2}
\frac{17.4}{1112}\\
&=
0.0036994,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
L_{\texttt{nano}}
&=
0.0003258
+
\frac{0.186}{1+0.2}
\frac{5+0.2\times105}{1112}\\
&=
0.0003258
+
\frac{0.186}{1.2}
\frac{26}{1112}\\
&=
0.0039499.
\end{aligned}
\]
The false-positive term is the same for both models. The choice therefore comes down to mini's higher inference cost and nano's higher false-negative cost. Mini adds about \(0.00095\) USD per request in API cost, but it avoids 43 false negatives on 1112 evaluated posts. That lowers the false-negative term by about \(0.00120\) USD per request, so mini ends up lower by about \(0.00025\) USD per request, or \(0.025\) USD per 100 categorizations.

\section{Scope of the decision}

The decision rule in Equation~\eqref{eq:model-selection} ranks the current candidate roster on the current common full-benchmark cases under declared penalties and observed inference charges. Small loss gaps therefore call for sensitivity reporting, close inspection of nearby decision boundaries, and a cautious deployment review rather than a claim of universal model superiority.
