\chapter{Data and Evaluation Setup}
\label{ch:data}

This chapter specifies the classification task, the reference benchmark, construction of the fixed monitoring subset $\mathcal{D}_n$ (with $n=300$), a size rationale aligned with the McNemar procedure in Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section}, and the interpretation of subset-based accuracy relative to the full benchmark. Repeated evaluation over time and the operational drift test are deferred to Section~\ref{sec:formalisation-temporal}.

\section{Formal problem definition}

Let $\mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the input space of social-media posts (text, metadata, visual features) and $\mathcal{Y}=\{0,1\}$ the binary label space ($1$: campaign-relevant). Under LLM-as-a-Service, the deployed classifier is a non-stationary mapping $f(x;\pi,\theta_t)\mapsto \hat{y}\in\{0,1\}$ with fixed prompt $\pi$ and provider-controlled weights $\theta_t$.

Under zero--one loss $\mathcal{L}(\hat{y},y)=\mathbb{I}(\hat{y}\neq y)$, the \textbf{population risk} at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
    R_t(f) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P_t}\bigl[\mathbb{I}\bigl(f(x;\pi,\theta_t)\neq y\bigr)\bigr],
\end{equation}
for a data-generating distribution $P_t$. The distribution $P_t$ is not observed.

The thesis uses a \textbf{fixed labelled benchmark} $\mathcal{D}_N=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ with $N=1199$, treated as the reference population for offline evaluation. For any fixed $t$, the \textbf{benchmark accuracy}
\begin{equation}
    A_N(t) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{I}\bigl(f(x_i;\pi,\theta_t)=y_i\bigr)
\end{equation}
is the empirical fraction of correct predictions on $\mathcal{D}_N$. It is the estimand for full-benchmark runs. Unless $\mathcal{D}_N$ is a probability sample from $P_t$, $A_N(t)$ need not equal $1-R_t(f)$; the two objects are not identified from each other without additional assumptions. Subsequent sections use $A_N$ and subset accuracy as operational targets; $R_t$ motivates non-stationarity of $f(\cdot;\pi,\theta_t)$.

\section{Reference benchmark, operational constraint, and monitoring subset}
\label{sec:input-datasets}

Full-benchmark evaluation of every candidate at high frequency is prohibitively expensive. Monitoring uses a fixed subset $\mathcal{D}_n\subset\mathcal{D}_N$ with $n\ll N$ on which the pipeline repeats inference; here $n=300$. The design goal is \emph{sensitivity} to changes in $\theta_t$, not unbiased estimation of $A_N(t)$ from $\mathcal{D}_n$ alone.

The sets $\mathcal{D}_N$ and $\mathcal{D}_n$ are stored as fixed Langfuse datasets with common schema and label column (\texttt{campaign\_relevant}). Table~\ref{tab:dataset-summary} summarises composition.

Each post is classified as an \textbf{image post} or a \textbf{video post} (multiple frames). Both sets are class-imbalanced; $\mathcal{D}_n$ is more positive-heavy than $\mathcal{D}_N$ (Table~\ref{tab:dataset-summary}), as a consequence of disagreement-based construction (Section~\ref{sec:informative-sampling-disagreement}). Accuracy on $\mathcal{D}_N$ and on $\mathcal{D}_n$ are not comparable by magnitude alone: prevalence and difficulty mix differ.

Roughly three quarters of items are video posts in both splits; multi-frame requests incur higher multimodal token cost than single-image posts. The subset is not a stratified copy of $\mathcal{D}_N$: it has a slightly larger image-post fraction yet a higher mean images per post ($5.87$ vs.\ $4.93$), so monitored runs are on average visually heavier. Empirical metrics in later chapters are reported on the fixed $\mathcal{D}_n$ unless stated otherwise.

\section{Disagreement-based subset construction}
\label{sec:informative-sampling-disagreement}

A simple random sample (SRS) of size $n$ from $\mathcal{D}_N$ yields an unbiased estimator of the \textbf{benchmark accuracy} $A_N(t)$ on $\mathcal{D}_N$ viewed as a finite population (simple random sampling without replacement). It does not, without further structure, target $R_t(f)$ under unknown $P_t$. Most benchmark items are easy for a given model ensemble; they show little cross-model variance and are insensitive to small shifts in $\theta_t$.

To increase sensitivity to provider-side changes, $\mathcal{D}_n$ is built from \textbf{query-by-committee} disagreement: instances on which an ensemble of distinct models disagrees. Let $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ens}}=\{f^{(1)},\ldots,f^{(K)}\}$ denote $K$ models evaluated on $\mathcal{D}_N$, and let $\hat{y}_{i}^{(k)}$ denote the predicted label for item $i$ by $f^{(k)}$. Define
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{n} = \left\{ x_i \in \mathcal{D}_{N} \;\middle|\; \exists\, k,\ell:\; \hat{y}_{i}^{(k)} \neq \hat{y}_{i}^{(\ell)} \right\},
\end{equation}
with a fixed selection rule on this pool yielding $n=300$ instances (the implementation fixes the disagreeing pool and its size). The objective is drift sensitivity, not minimum-variance estimation of $A_N(t)$ on $\mathcal{D}_n$.

\subsection{Geometry of the decision boundary}

In representation learning it is common to assume the \emph{manifold hypothesis}: nominally high-dimensional data concentrate near a lower-dimensional submanifold~\cite{cayton2005,fefferman2016}, which supports inductive bias in deep models~\cite{bengio2013}.

\begin{assumption}[Data manifold]
The input space $\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ is a smooth, compact Riemannian manifold.
\end{assumption}

For an ensemble $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ens}}$, let $p_m:\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]$ denote a smooth scoring function for model $m$ (e.g.\ a calibrated positive-class probability or a sigmoid of a logit), and define the \emph{consensus} function
\begin{equation}
    \bar{p}(x) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{m=1}^{K} p_m(x).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Consensus decision boundary]
The ensemble decision boundary is the level set
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{B} = \bar{p}^{-1}(0.5) = \left\{ x\in\mathcal{X} \mid \bar{p}(x)=0.5 \right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
If $0.5$ is a regular value of $\bar{p}$ (i.e.\ $\nabla \bar{p}(x)\neq 0$ for all $x\in\mathcal{B}$), then by the regular level set theorem, $\mathcal{B}$ is a smooth submanifold of $\mathcal{X}$ of codimension~$1$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Disagreement and proximity to \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{B}$}{the boundary}}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded ensemble divergence]
For all $m\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$,
\begin{equation}
    \sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}} \left| p_m(x) - \bar{p}(x) \right| \le \epsilon.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Disagreement bounds distance to $0.5$ in $\bar{p}$]
If for some $x$ there exist $j,k$ with $p_j(x)<0.5$ and $p_k(x)\ge 0.5$, then $\bar{p}(x)\in[0.5-\epsilon,\,0.5+\epsilon]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the assumption, $\bar{p}(x)-\epsilon \le p_j(x) < 0.5$ implies $\bar{p}(x) < 0.5+\epsilon$, and $\bar{p}(x)+\epsilon \ge p_k(x) \ge 0.5$ implies $\bar{p}(x) \ge 0.5-\epsilon$.
\end{proof}

Thus ensemble disagreement isolates instances in an $\epsilon$-neighbourhood of the consensus threshold in $\bar{p}$: geometrically, a tube around $\mathcal{B}$. In the limit $\epsilon\to 0$, the Bernoulli variance $A(1-A)$ for a hard decision at that threshold is maximal ($0.25$ at $A=0.5$), which motivates pairing disagreement-based construction with the sample-size discussion in Section~\ref{sec:monitoring-sample-size}.

\subsection{Disagreement score}

For each $x_i\in\mathcal{D}_N$, let
\begin{equation}
    r_i = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbb{I}\!\left(\hat{y}_{i}^{(k)}=1\right),
    \qquad
    d_i = 4r_i(1-r_i)\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
Then $d_i=0$ under full agreement ($r_i\in\{0,1\}$) and $d_i$ is maximal at $r_i=\tfrac{1}{2}$. The score $d_i$ measures cross-model controversy on item $i$.

\subsection{Temporal flips as a design target}

Let $F_i(t_1,t_2)\in\{0,1\}$ indicate that the \emph{correctness} of the deployed classifier on item $i$ differs between monitoring dates $t_1<t_2$. Section~\ref{sec:formalisation-temporal} defines correctness indicators $E_{i,t}$; equivalently $F_i(t_1,t_2)=\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}\neq E_{i,t_2})$ for the deployment under study.

\begin{proposition}[Higher disagreement and expected flips]
\label{prop:disagreement-flips}
If $P(F_i(t_1,t_2)=1\mid d_i)$ is non-decreasing in $d_i$, then for every $\tau\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\bigl[F_i(t_1,t_2)\mid d_i\ge \tau\bigr] \ge \mathbb{E}\bigl[F_i(t_1,t_2)\mid d_i<\tau\bigr].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from the monotonicity assumption and the fact that conditioning on $d_i\ge\tau$ shifts mass toward larger $d_i$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is conditional: it does not assert representativeness of $\mathcal{D}_n$ for $\mathcal{D}_N$. It motivates enriching the monitoring panel with high-$d_i$ items so that paired tests (Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section}) encounter more discordant correctness pairs when $\theta_t$ shifts.

\section{Monitoring subset size}
\label{sec:monitoring-sample-size}

Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section} defines discordant counts $b$ (regressions) and $c$ (improvements) between $t_1$ and $t_2$ and the McNemar statistic. For \emph{design}, let $p_b=P(E_{i,t_1}=1,E_{i,t_2}=0)$ and $p_c=P(E_{i,t_1}=0,E_{i,t_2}=1)$ under the monitoring distribution on $\mathcal{D}_n$. Write $\rho=p_b+p_c$ for the discordance probability per item and $\delta=p_b-p_c$ for directional imbalance. On $\mathcal{D}_n$,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_{t_2}-\hat{A}_{t_1}=\frac{c-b}{n}
\end{equation}
(see Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section}). Detecting a benchmark-relevant drop in accuracy of at least $\Delta_{\mathrm{acc}}=0.05$ on the panel is related to $\delta$ and $\rho$; McNemar's power rises when $\rho$ is larger (more discordant pairs for fixed $n$).

The choice $\Delta_{\mathrm{acc}}=0.05$ is tied to the \textbf{minimum detectable effect} relative to label noise. In production annotation, human inter-annotator disagreement on borderline posts induces an irreducible error rate on the order of $\epsilon_{\mathrm{noise}}\approx 0.05$; drifts smaller than that are not reliably distinguishable from background labelling variability. The planning calculation below therefore treats $\Delta_{\mathrm{acc}}=0.05$ as an operational margin aligned with that floor, not as a universal statistical identity.

A normal-approximation sample-size requirement for a one-sided level-$\alpha$ test with power $1-\beta$ takes the form
\begin{equation}
    n \ge \frac{1}{\rho} \left(
        \frac{
            z_{1-\alpha}\sqrt{0.25} + z_{1-\beta}\sqrt{\pi(1-\pi)}
        }{
            \pi-0.5
        }
    \right)^2,
    \qquad
    \pi = \frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2\rho},
\end{equation}
with $\pi$ the conditional probability of a regression given discordance. Plausible planning ranges $\rho\in[0.10,0.15]$ on $\mathcal{D}_n$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{acc}}=0.05$ imply $\pi\in[0.667,0.75]$. For $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.05,0.2)$, the implied discordant count $m=n\rho$ is about $23$--$29$, hence $n\approx m/\rho\in[190,290]$. Setting $n=300$ is a conservative operational choice yielding roughly $30$--$45$ discordant pairs when $\rho$ lies in that range, sufficient for one-sided McNemar-style alerts at $\Delta_{\mathrm{acc}}\approx 0.05$ under the stated regime. This is a planning value, not a universal lower bound.

\section{Selection bias and importance weighting}
\label{sec:selection-bias}

Because $\mathcal{D}_n$ oversamples high-$d_i$ instances, the raw subset accuracy
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_n(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_n}\mathbb{I}\bigl(\hat{y}_{i,t}=y_i\bigr)
\end{equation}
is generally biased for $A_N(t)$: the estimands differ. The design \emph{accepts} that bias to amplify instability where ensemble disagreement is large. Temporal shifts in provider weights $\theta_t$ are expected to surface first on such items, improving power of the paired monitor relative to a uniform $\mathcal{D}_n$.

Relating $\hat{A}_n$ to $A_N$ by inverse probability weighting requires a sampling design with known inclusion probabilities $q_i=P(i\in\mathcal{D}_n)>0$ for all $i\in\mathcal{D}_N$. The implemented rule is deterministic disagreement-based selection with $q_i=0$ for many $i$; Horvitz--Thompson (HT) recovery of $A_N$ from $\mathcal{D}_n$ alone is therefore not defined.

\subsection{Horvitz--Thompson estimator under a positive design}

If, hypothetically, $S_i\in\{0,1\}$ indicated inclusion with known $q_i>0$ for all $i=1,\ldots,N$, then
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_{\mathrm{HT}}(t) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{S_i\,\mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_{i,t}=y_i)}{q_i}
\end{equation}
is unbiased for $A_N(t)$ over the sampling design, with $\hat{y}_{i,t}$ fixed at $t$.

\begin{proof}
$\mathbb{E}[\hat{A}_{\mathrm{HT}}(t)] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_{i,t}=y_i)\,\mathbb{E}[S_i]/q_i = A_N(t)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Monitoring objective and signal amplification}

The Horvitz--Thompson construction recovers $A_N(t)$ by reweighting included instances inversely with $q_i$, thereby mapping the monitoring measure back toward the uniform benchmark measure. That reweighting \emph{down-weights} frequent error types on the disagreement-enriched panel when their inclusion probabilities are low: it is appropriate for unbiased \emph{population} estimation.

For drift \emph{detection}, the goal is different: maximise sensitivity to temporal changes in $\theta_t$ where the classifier is unstable. Large importance weights $1/q_i$ would suppress the contribution of boundary-heavy errors to the aggregate score, ``flattening'' the signal toward the global mean and reducing contrast for anomaly monitoring. Reporting the raw biased empirical accuracy $\hat{A}_n(t)$ (and confusion structure on $\mathcal{D}_n$) therefore acts as a high-gain monitoring signal on the fixed panel; the HT framework above still formalises how a positively designed subset would link to $A_N(t)$ if unbiased recovery were required.

The thesis uses unweighted $\hat{A}_n(t)$ and related confusion-based metrics on $\mathcal{D}_n$ (Section~\ref{sec:classification-metrics}) as leading indicators of drift, not as HT estimates of $A_N(t)$. If unbiased estimation of $A_N(t)$ alongside drift monitoring were required, one would add a separate random component (e.g.\ SRS from $\mathcal{D}_N$) with positive $q_i$ on all items of interest.
