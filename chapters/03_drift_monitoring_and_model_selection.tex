\chapter{Drift Monitoring and Model Selection Methodology}
\label{ch:drift}

Chapter~\ref{ch:data} fixes the monitoring panel $\mathcal{D}_n=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ with $n=300$. This chapter specifies repeated evaluation on $\mathcal{D}_n$, paired degradation detection via McNemar's test \cite{mcnemar1947}, classification metrics for reporting and candidate comparison, and the decision logic linking degradation evidence to corrective action.

\section{Temporal evaluation on a fixed panel}
\label{sec:formalisation-temporal}

Let $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ index discrete evaluation batches (e.g.\ weekly). At each $t$, each model under study is evaluated on the same $\mathcal{D}_n$. Cross-time differences therefore reflect changes in outputs on a fixed set of items rather than resampling of the evaluation panel. This removes panel-resampling variation, but cross-time differences may still reflect provider-side model updates, inference-time nondeterminism, or other operational variability.

For instance $i$ and time $t$, let $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$ be the predicted label and define the correctness indicator
\begin{equation}
    E_{i,t} = \mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_{i,t}=y_i).
\end{equation}

For any candidate model $m\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cand}}$, let $\hat{y}_{i,t}(m)$ denote model $m$'s prediction on instance $i$ at time $t$. Define empirical accuracy on $\mathcal{D}_n$ by
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_t(m) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{I}\bigl(\hat{y}_{i,t}(m)=y_i\bigr).
\end{equation}
For the current deployment $m_{\mathrm{curr}}$, write
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_t:=\hat{A}_t(m_{\mathrm{curr}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i,t}.
\end{equation}

The sequence $(\hat{A}_t)_{t=1}^T$ is not a sequence of independent Bernoulli means, because the same items are reused across time. Independent-sample comparisons of $\hat{A}_{t_1}$ and $\hat{A}_{t_2}$, such as two-proportion $z$-tests, do not use the correct matched-pair variance and are therefore inappropriate here. Degradation detection instead uses the paired structure through $(E_{i,t_1},E_{i,t_2})$ (Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section}).

\section{Statistical degradation detection via McNemar's test}
\label{sec:mcnemar-section}

The goal is to detect whether the deployed model became worse between two evaluation times $t_1$ and $t_2$ on the fixed monitoring panel $\mathcal{D}_n$.

Because the same items are evaluated at both times, the comparison is paired. For each item, the model is either correct or wrong at $t_1$, and either correct or wrong at $t_2$. McNemar's test \cite{mcnemar1947} then compares two kinds of changes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item regressions: correct at $t_1$, wrong at $t_2$;
    \item improvements: wrong at $t_1$, correct at $t_2$.
\end{itemize}

If regressions clearly outnumber improvements, this is evidence of degradation. If the numbers are similar, the observed change is treated as noise rather than a clear deterioration.

The test establishes degradation on $\mathcal{D}_n$ only. By Section~\ref{sec:selection-bias}, that is a high-sensitivity warning, not a claim about $\mathcal{D}_N$ or production.

\subsection{Contingency table}

Compare $(E_{i,t_1},E_{i,t_2})$ for each $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$. The four outcomes partition $\mathcal{D}_n$:

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption[Paired correctness table]{Paired $2\times2$ correctness at $t_1$ and $t_2$.}
    \label{tab:mcnemar_contingency}
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        & $E_{i,t_2}=1$ & $E_{i,t_2}=0$ \\
        \hline
        $E_{i,t_1}=1$
            & $\displaystyle a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=1,\;E_{i,t_2}=1)$
            & $\displaystyle b=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=1,\;E_{i,t_2}=0)$ \\
        $E_{i,t_1}=0$
            & $\displaystyle c=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=0,\;E_{i,t_2}=1)$
            & $\displaystyle d=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=0,\;E_{i,t_2}=0)$
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Then
\begin{equation}
    a+b+c+d=n.
\end{equation}
Concordant cells $a$ and $d$ carry no information on directional change; evidence is in the discordant cells $b$ (regressions) and $c$ (improvements). Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_{t_1}=\frac{a+b}{n},
    \qquad
    \hat{A}_{t_2}=\frac{a+c}{n},
    \qquad
    \hat{A}_{t_2}-\hat{A}_{t_1}=\frac{c-b}{n}.
\end{equation}

Thus the panel-level accuracy drop is determined by the net imbalance between regressions and improvements.

\subsection{Exact conditional test and alerting rule}

Under the panel-level stochastic model, let $p_b$ and $p_c$ denote the item-level probabilities of falling into the two discordant cells:
\[
p_b=P(E_{i,t_1}=1,E_{i,t_2}=0),
\qquad
p_c=P(E_{i,t_1}=0,E_{i,t_2}=1).
\]
When degradation is the operational concern, McNemar's test is formulated as
\[
H_0:p_b=p_c
\qquad\text{against}\qquad
H_a:p_b>p_c.
\]

Let $n_{\mathrm{dis}}=b+c$ denote the number of discordant pairs. Conditional on $n_{\mathrm{dis}}>0$, and under $H_0$, each discordant pair is equally likely to be a regression or an improvement \cite{mcnemar1947}, so by~\eqref{eq:conditional-binomial}
\begin{equation}
    B:=b \mid (b+c=n_{\mathrm{dis}})\sim \mathrm{Bin}\!\left(n_{\mathrm{dis}},\tfrac{1}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
The exact one-sided p-value for degradation is
\begin{equation}
    p_{\mathrm{exact}}
    =
    P\!\left(B\ge b_{\mathrm{obs}}\right)
    =
    \sum_{k=b_{\mathrm{obs}}}^{n_{\mathrm{dis}}}\binom{n_{\mathrm{dis}}}{k}2^{-n_{\mathrm{dis}}}.
\end{equation}

For reference, one may also report the continuity-corrected asymptotic McNemar statistic
\begin{equation}
    \chi^2_{\mathrm{cc}}=\frac{(|b-c|-1)^2}{b+c},
\end{equation}
defined when $b+c>0$. This is a large-sample approximation based on the discordant counts only. Large values indicate a stronger imbalance between regressions and improvements. However, $\chi^2_{\mathrm{cc}}$ is non-directional because it depends on $|b-c|$, so it does not distinguish degradation from improvement by itself. It is therefore reported only as a descriptive reference statistic. Different variants of the McNemar test, including exact conditional and asymptotic forms, are discussed in \cite{fagerland2013mcnemar}. Alerting in this thesis uses the exact one-sided p-value because discordant counts can be small on the fixed 300-item panel.

A degradation alert is registered when both of the following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $b>c$;
    \item $p_{\mathrm{exact}}<\alpha=0.05$.
\end{enumerate}

Condition (i) enforces the direction of interest. Condition (ii) provides statistical evidence against equal probabilities of regressions and improvements.

If $b+c=0$, there are no discordant pairs and no directional conclusion is drawn.

Because the monitoring rule is applied repeatedly over time, $\alpha=0.05$ should be interpreted as a \emph{per-comparison} threshold rather than as a long-run false-alert guarantee. Under repeated monitoring, cumulative false-alert probability increases over time.

\subsection{Baseline choice and multiplicity control}
\label{sec:baseline-multiplicity}

Two operational details fix how the test in Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section} is applied over time.

\paragraph{Fixed baseline.}
Each model is compared against its \emph{earliest} available run on $\mathcal{D}_n$, not against the preceding week. Write $t_0$ for that first run and $t_1,\dots,t_{T-1}$ for the later ones. Every alert is then a test of $t_k$ against $t_0$. Consecutive-week testing would compare two runs that may both already be degraded, so a slow step down would never produce a large discordant imbalance in any single week. Anchoring on $t_0$ keeps the comparison against the state the deployment was accepted in.

The cost is a dependence on one batch. Because every comparison reuses $t_0$, ordinary sampling noise in that single run shifts the whole series in one direction. Section~\ref{sec:drift-results} shows a case where this happens.

\paragraph{Multiplicity.}
With $T-1$ comparisons per model, each tested at $\alpha$, the probability of at least one false alert grows as $1-(1-\alpha)^{T-1}$ under independence: for $T-1=22$ and $\alpha=0.05$ this is about $0.68$. A Bonferroni correction restores family-wise control at level $\alpha$ by testing each comparison at
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\mathrm{adj}}=\frac{\alpha}{T-1}.
    \label{eq:bonferroni}
\end{equation}
For $T-1=22$ this gives $\alpha_{\mathrm{adj}}\approx 0.0023$. The correction is applied per model, over that model's own comparisons, since alerts are acted on per deployment.

Bonferroni is used rather than the step-down Holm procedure because the alert is evaluated online: at time $t_k$ the p-values of later comparisons do not yet exist, and Holm needs the whole family ranked before any threshold is fixed. Bonferroni gives a threshold that is known in advance and is therefore usable in a weekly job. It is the more conservative of the two.

The alerting rule of Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section} is used unchanged, with $\alpha$ replaced by $\alpha_{\mathrm{adj}}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $b>c$;
    \item $p_{\mathrm{exact}}<\alpha_{\mathrm{adj}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Classification metrics on the monitoring subset}
\label{sec:classification-metrics}

All metrics below are computed on $\mathcal{D}_n$. Levels and rankings need not match $\mathcal{D}_N$ (Section~\ref{sec:selection-bias}).

Let $\mathrm{TP}_t$, $\mathrm{FP}_t$, $\mathrm{TN}_t$, and $\mathrm{FN}_t$ be confusion counts on $\mathcal{D}_n$ at time $t$ for a given model and a fixed rule mapping provider output to $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$. Omit any ratio whose denominator is zero.

\paragraph{Accuracy.}
Empirical accuracy is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{TN}_t}
         {\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{TN}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t+\mathrm{FN}_t}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed model and parsing rule, this equals $\hat{A}_t$. It summarises global correctness but weights errors by class prevalence on $\mathcal{D}_n$.

\paragraph{Precision, recall, and $F_1$.}
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t}{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t}{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{FN}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{F}}_{1,t}
    =
    \frac{2\,\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t\,\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t}
         {\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t}.
\end{equation}
Precision is positive predictive value; recall is sensitivity on the positive class. The $F_1$ score is their harmonic mean when both are defined and $\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t>0$.

\paragraph{Specificity and balanced accuracy.}
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Spec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TN}_t}{\mathrm{TN}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{BA}}_t
    =
    \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Spec}}_t\bigr).
\end{equation}
Specificity measures true-negative performance. Balanced accuracy mitigates inflation of raw accuracy under prevalence skew on the panel.

\paragraph{Hard labels only.}
The monitoring pipeline maps provider outputs to a single hard label $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$ via a fixed parsing rule; no per-example graded score is logged for the experiments reported here. The metrics above are therefore computed from one confusion table per model and batch. 

\paragraph{Which metric is most diagnostic on the monitoring panel.}
Prevalence on $\mathcal{D}_n$ is high (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}), so raw accuracy can hide asymmetric errors. Prefer recall when FN is expensive, specificity when FP is expensive, and precision when analyst capacity is limited by false alarms. McNemar remains the inferential alert; confusion metrics catch class-specific shifts that net correctness can cancel.
\section{Model comparison and deployment choice}
\label{sec:model-comparison-manual}

Candidates can be screened on $\mathcal{D}_n$ with the metrics above and mean inference cost $C_t(m)$. Final deployment ranking still uses $\mathcal{D}_N$ under Chapter~\ref{ch:cost-deployment-utility} (Section~\ref{sec:selection-bias}). Operationally: detect on $\mathcal{D}_n$ with the one-sided McNemar rule; if action is required, compare on $\mathcal{D}_N$ (cost-aware or constrained), optionally with prompt repair before a model switch (Chapter~\ref{chap:prompt-optimisation}).
