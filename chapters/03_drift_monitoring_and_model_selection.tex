\chapter{Drift Monitoring and Model Selection Methodology}
\label{ch:drift}

Chapter~\ref{ch:data} fixes the monitoring panel $\mathcal{D}_n=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ with $n=300$. This chapter specifies repeated evaluation on $\mathcal{D}_n$, paired drift detection via McNemar's test, classification metrics for reporting and candidate comparison, and the decision logic linking drift detection to corrective action. A cost-based expected-cost utility for deployment ranking is developed in Chapter~\ref{ch:cost-deployment-utility}. The estimand is change in deployment behaviour on the fixed monitoring panel $\mathcal{D}_n$; it does not identify the population risk $R_t(f)$ from Chapter~\ref{ch:data} without additional assumptions.

\section{Temporal evaluation on a fixed panel}
\label{sec:formalisation-temporal}

Let $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ index discrete evaluation batches (e.g.\ weekly). At each $t$, each model under study is evaluated on the same $\mathcal{D}_n$. Cross-time differences therefore reflect changes in outputs on a fixed set of items, not resampling of the evaluation panel.

For instance $i$ and time $t$, let $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$ be the predicted label and
\begin{equation}
    E_{i,t} = \mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_{i,t}=y_i)
\end{equation}
the correctness indicator.

For any $m\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cand}}$, let $\hat{y}_{i,t}(m)$ denote model $m$'s prediction on instance $i$ at time $t$. Define empirical accuracy on $\mathcal{D}_n$ by
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_t(m) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{I}\bigl(\hat{y}_{i,t}(m)=y_i\bigr).
\end{equation}
For the current deployment $m_{\mathrm{curr}}$, write
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_t:=\hat{A}_t(m_{\mathrm{curr}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i,t}.
\end{equation}

The sequence $(\hat{A}_t)_{t=1}^T$ is not a sequence of independent Bernoulli means: the same items are reused across time. Marginal comparisons of $\hat{A}_{t_1}$ and $\hat{A}_{t_2}$ that assume independence (e.g.\ two-proportion $z$-tests) therefore overstate precision. Drift detection uses the paired structure through $(E_{i,t_1},E_{i,t_2})$ (Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section}).

\section{Statistical drift detection via McNemar's test}
\label{sec:mcnemar-section}

Drift analysis targets per-instance changes in correctness between times $t_1$ and $t_2$, not merely the marginal difference $\hat{A}_{t_2}-\hat{A}_{t_1}$.

\subsection{Contingency table}

Compare $(E_{i,t_1},E_{i,t_2})$ for each $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$. The four outcomes partition $\mathcal{D}_n$:

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Paired $2\times2$ table for correctness at $t_1$ and $t_2$.}
    \label{tab:mcnemar_contingency}
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        & $E_{i,t_2}=1$ & $E_{i,t_2}=0$ \\
        \hline
        $E_{i,t_1}=1$
            & $\displaystyle a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=1,\;E_{i,t_2}=1)$
            & $\displaystyle b=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=1,\;E_{i,t_2}=0)$ \\
        $E_{i,t_1}=0$
            & $\displaystyle c=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=0,\;E_{i,t_2}=1)$
            & $\displaystyle d=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=0,\;E_{i,t_2}=0)$
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Then
\begin{equation}
    a+b+c+d=n.
\end{equation}
Concordant cells $a$ and $d$ carry no information on directional change; evidence is in the discordant cells $b$ (regressions) and $c$ (improvements). Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_{t_1}=\frac{a+b}{n},
    \qquad
    \hat{A}_{t_2}=\frac{a+c}{n},
    \qquad
    \hat{A}_{t_2}-\hat{A}_{t_1}=\frac{c-b}{n}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact conditional test and alerting rule}

Let $p_b$ and $p_c$ denote the operational probabilities of a regression and an improvement on the fixed monitoring panel:
\[
p_b=P(E_{i,t_1}=1,E_{i,t_2}=0),
\qquad
p_c=P(E_{i,t_1}=0,E_{i,t_2}=1).
\]
When degradation is the operational concern, McNemar's test is formulated as
\[
H_0:p_b=p_c
\qquad\text{against}\qquad
H_a:p_b>p_c.
\]

Let
\[
n_{\mathrm{dis}}=b+c
\]
denote the number of discordant pairs. Conditional on $n_{\mathrm{dis}}>0$, the exact McNemar test uses the fact that under $H_0$ each discordant pair is equally likely to be a regression or an improvement. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    B:=b \mid (b+c=n_{\mathrm{dis}})\sim \mathrm{Bin}\!\left(n_{\mathrm{dis}},\tfrac{1}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
The exact one-sided p-value for degradation is then
\begin{equation}
    p_{\mathrm{exact}}
    =
    P\!\left(B\ge b_{\mathrm{obs}}\right)
    =
    \sum_{k=b_{\mathrm{obs}}}^{n_{\mathrm{dis}}}\binom{n_{\mathrm{dis}}}{k}2^{-n_{\mathrm{dis}}},
    \qquad \text{when } b_{\mathrm{obs}}>c_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\end{equation}

For reference, the continuity-corrected asymptotic statistic is
\begin{equation}
    \chi^2_{\mathrm{cc}}=\frac{(|b-c|-1)^2}{b+c},
\end{equation}
defined when $b+c>0$. This statistic is a large-sample approximation; alerting in this thesis uses the exact one-sided p-value because discordant counts can be small on the fixed 300-item panel.

A statistical drift alert is registered when
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $b>c$, and
    \item $p_{\mathrm{exact}}<\alpha=0.05$.
\end{enumerate}
Condition (i) excludes net improvements. If $b+c=0$, there are no discordant pairs and no directional conclusion is drawn.

\subsection{Empirical examples from monitoring runs}

Tables~\ref{tab:mcnemar_gpt5_meth} and~\ref{tab:mcnemar_gpt5mini_meth} show paired contingency tables from two batch comparisons on the $n=300$ monitoring subset (8~March~2026 vs.\ 10~March~2026).

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{McNemar table for \texttt{gpt-5} between 8~March~2026 and 10~March~2026 on $\mathcal{D}_n$.}
    \label{tab:mcnemar_gpt5_meth}
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        & 2026-03-10 correct & 2026-03-10 wrong \\
        \hline
        2026-03-08 correct & 291 & 0 \\
        2026-03-08 wrong   & 0   & 9 \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Here $(b,c)=(0,0)$, so there are no discordant pairs. Operationally, no drift alert is issued and no directional change is detected on the fixed panel.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{McNemar table for \texttt{gpt-5.1-mini} between 8~March~2026 and 10~March~2026 on $\mathcal{D}_n$.}
    \label{tab:mcnemar_gpt5mini_meth}
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        & 2026-03-10 correct & 2026-03-10 wrong \\
        \hline
        2026-03-08 correct & 269 & 13 \\
        2026-03-08 wrong   & 7   & 11 \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Here $(b,c)=(13,7)$, so $n_{\mathrm{dis}}=20$ and
\[
    p_{\mathrm{exact}}
    =
    \sum_{k=13}^{20}\binom{20}{k}2^{-20}
    \approx 0.132.
\]
As a large-sample summary,
\[
    \chi^2_{\mathrm{cc}}
    =
    \frac{(|13-7|-1)^2}{20}
    =
    \frac{25}{20}
    =
    1.25.
\]
Although $b>c$, the one-sided exact evidence does not meet $\alpha=0.05$, so no statistical drift alert is triggered under the rule above.

\section{Classification metrics on the monitoring subset}
\label{sec:classification-metrics}

All metrics below are computed on $\mathcal{D}_n$ (Section~\ref{sec:informative-sampling-disagreement}). They describe behaviour on the disagreement-enriched panel: ranks and levels need not match full-benchmark $A_N(t)$ (Section~\ref{sec:selection-bias}).

Let $\mathrm{TP}_t$, $\mathrm{FP}_t$, $\mathrm{TN}_t$, and $\mathrm{FN}_t$ be confusion counts on $\mathcal{D}_n$ at time $t$ for a given model and a fixed rule mapping provider output to $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$. Omit any ratio whose denominator is zero.

\paragraph{Accuracy.}
Empirical accuracy is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{TN}_t}
         {\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{TN}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t+\mathrm{FN}_t}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed model and parsing rule, this equals $\hat{A}_t$. It summarises global correctness but weights errors by class prevalence on $\mathcal{D}_n$.

\paragraph{Precision, recall, and $F_1$.}
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t}{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t}{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{FN}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{F}}_{1,t}
    =
    \frac{2\,\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t\,\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t}
         {\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t}.
\end{equation}
Precision is positive predictive value; recall is sensitivity on the positive class. The $F_1$ score is their harmonic mean when both are defined and $\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t>0$.

\paragraph{Specificity and balanced accuracy.}
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Spec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TN}_t}{\mathrm{TN}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{BA}}_t
    =
    \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Spec}}_t\bigr).
\end{equation}
Specificity measures true-negative performance. Balanced accuracy mitigates inflation of raw accuracy under prevalence skew on the panel.

\paragraph{Hard labels only.}
The monitoring pipeline maps provider outputs to a single hard label $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$ via a fixed parsing rule; no per-example graded score is logged for the experiments reported here. The metrics above are therefore computed from one confusion table per model and batch. Threshold-sweep summaries such as precision--recall curves or PR--AUC are not defined on these hard labels and are omitted. Drift alerts remain based on McNemar on hard correctness (Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section}).

\paragraph{When to trust which metric.}
On the disagreement-enriched panel, prevalence is high (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}), so raw accuracy can hide asymmetric errors. Recall should dominate when missed relevant posts are expensive (high FN burden), while specificity should dominate when over-flagging irrelevant posts is operationally costly (high FP burden). Precision is useful when analyst workload is constrained by false alarms. McNemar remains the inferential test for drift alerts.

\section{Model comparison and deployment choice}
\label{sec:model-comparison-manual}

Candidate models $m\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cand}}$ may first be screened on $\mathcal{D}_n$ using $\hat{A}_t(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{F}}_{1,t}(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{Spec}}_t(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{BA}}_t(m)$, and observed mean inference cost $C_t(m)$ to obtain a quick descriptive profile on the drift-sensitive panel.

However, because $\mathcal{D}_n$ is disagreement-enriched and strongly class-skewed, these subset-level quantities are not by themselves an appropriate basis for final deployment-cost estimation or constrained deployment selection. Final deployment choice under Chapter~\ref{ch:cost-deployment-utility} should therefore be made on the full benchmark $\mathcal{D}_N$ or another representative evaluation set.

Two decision layers are kept separate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item inferential detection of degradation for $m_{\mathrm{curr}}$ on the fixed monitoring panel $\mathcal{D}_n$ via the exact one-sided McNemar rule in Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section};
    \item if action is required, full-benchmark comparison of candidate models using the cost-aware or constrained rules from Chapter~\ref{ch:cost-deployment-utility}.
\end{enumerate}

Campaigns differ in the relative cost of false positives and false negatives. While Chapter~\ref{ch:cost-deployment-utility} presents a cost-based utility as one formalisation of the accuracy--cost trade-off, final deployment decisions incorporate manual judgment alongside these quantitative criteria (tolerance to error types, budget, latency, and provider policy). If no acceptable fallback exists, prompt optimisation may be attempted before changing the API model (Chapter~\ref{chap:prompt-optimisation}). Exported numeric panels and dashboards used in experiments are described in Chapter~\ref{chap:system-architecture}.
