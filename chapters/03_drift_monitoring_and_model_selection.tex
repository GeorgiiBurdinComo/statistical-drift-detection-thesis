\chapter{Drift Monitoring and Model Selection Methodology}
\label{ch:drift}

Chapter~\ref{ch:data} fixes the monitoring panel $\mathcal{D}_n=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ with $n=300$. This chapter specifies repeated evaluation on $\mathcal{D}_n$, paired degradation detection via McNemar's test, classification metrics for reporting and candidate comparison, and the decision logic linking degradation evidence to corrective action. 

\section{Temporal evaluation on a fixed panel}
\label{sec:formalisation-temporal}

Let $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ index discrete evaluation batches (e.g.\ weekly). At each $t$, each model under study is evaluated on the same $\mathcal{D}_n$. Cross-time differences therefore reflect changes in outputs on a fixed set of items rather than resampling of the evaluation panel. This removes panel-resampling variation, but cross-time differences may still reflect provider-side model updates, inference-time nondeterminism, or other operational variability.

For instance $i$ and time $t$, let $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$ be the predicted label and define the correctness indicator
\begin{equation}
    E_{i,t} = \mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_{i,t}=y_i).
\end{equation}

For any candidate model $m\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cand}}$, let $\hat{y}_{i,t}(m)$ denote model $m$'s prediction on instance $i$ at time $t$. Define empirical accuracy on $\mathcal{D}_n$ by
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_t(m) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{I}\bigl(\hat{y}_{i,t}(m)=y_i\bigr).
\end{equation}
For the current deployment $m_{\mathrm{curr}}$, write
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_t:=\hat{A}_t(m_{\mathrm{curr}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i,t}.
\end{equation}

The sequence $(\hat{A}_t)_{t=1}^T$ is not a sequence of independent Bernoulli means, because the same items are reused across time. Independent-sample comparisons of $\hat{A}_{t_1}$ and $\hat{A}_{t_2}$, such as two-proportion $z$-tests, do not use the correct matched-pair variance and are therefore inappropriate here. Degradation detection instead uses the paired structure through $(E_{i,t_1},E_{i,t_2})$ (Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section}).

\section{Statistical degradation detection via McNemar's test}
\label{sec:mcnemar-section}

The operational alert targets \emph{net degradation in correctness} on the fixed monitoring panel between times $t_1$ and $t_2$. It is therefore more specific than a generic notion of behavioural drift: balanced churn or net improvement need not trigger an alert even if many individual items change status.

Inferentially, the test treats the $n$ item-level paired correctness outcomes on the fixed panel as approximately independent operational units. The resulting $p$-values quantify evidence of directional change on this panel; they do not, by themselves, establish a population-level change under the full post distribution.

\subsection{Contingency table}

Compare $(E_{i,t_1},E_{i,t_2})$ for each $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$. The four outcomes partition $\mathcal{D}_n$:

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Paired $2\times2$ table for correctness at $t_1$ and $t_2$.}
    \label{tab:mcnemar_contingency}
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        & $E_{i,t_2}=1$ & $E_{i,t_2}=0$ \\
        \hline
        $E_{i,t_1}=1$
            & $\displaystyle a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=1,\;E_{i,t_2}=1)$
            & $\displaystyle b=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=1,\;E_{i,t_2}=0)$ \\
        $E_{i,t_1}=0$
            & $\displaystyle c=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=0,\;E_{i,t_2}=1)$
            & $\displaystyle d=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}(E_{i,t_1}=0,\;E_{i,t_2}=0)$
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Then
\begin{equation}
    a+b+c+d=n.
\end{equation}
Concordant cells $a$ and $d$ carry no information on directional change; evidence is in the discordant cells $b$ (regressions) and $c$ (improvements). Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_{t_1}=\frac{a+b}{n},
    \qquad
    \hat{A}_{t_2}=\frac{a+c}{n},
    \qquad
    \hat{A}_{t_2}-\hat{A}_{t_1}=\frac{c-b}{n}.
\end{equation}

Thus the panel-level accuracy drop is determined by the net imbalance between regressions and improvements.

\subsection{Exact conditional test and alerting rule}

Let $p_b$ and $p_c$ denote the operational probabilities of a regression and an improvement on the fixed monitoring panel:
\[
p_b=P(E_{i,t_1}=1,E_{i,t_2}=0),
\qquad
p_c=P(E_{i,t_1}=0,E_{i,t_2}=1).
\]
When degradation is the operational concern, McNemar's test is formulated as
\[
H_0:p_b=p_c
\qquad\text{against}\qquad
H_a:p_b>p_c.
\]

Let
\[
n_{\mathrm{dis}}=b+c
\]
denote the number of discordant pairs. Conditional on $n_{\mathrm{dis}}>0$, the exact McNemar test uses the fact that under $H_0$ each discordant pair is equally likely to be a regression or an improvement. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    B:=b \mid (b+c=n_{\mathrm{dis}})\sim \mathrm{Bin}\!\left(n_{\mathrm{dis}},\tfrac{1}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
For the one-sided degradation test, define
\begin{equation}
    p_{\mathrm{exact}}
    =
    P\!\left(B\ge b_{\mathrm{obs}}\right)
    =
    \sum_{k=b_{\mathrm{obs}}}^{n_{\mathrm{dis}}}\binom{n_{\mathrm{dis}}}{k}2^{-n_{\mathrm{dis}}},
    \qquad \text{when } b_{\mathrm{obs}}>c_{\mathrm{obs}},
\end{equation}
and set $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=1$ when $b_{\mathrm{obs}}\le c_{\mathrm{obs}}$.

For reference, the continuity-corrected asymptotic statistic is
\begin{equation}
    \chi^2_{\mathrm{cc}}=\frac{(|b-c|-1)^2}{b+c},
\end{equation}
defined when $b+c>0$. This statistic is a large-sample approximation; alerting in this thesis uses the exact one-sided $p$-value because discordant counts can be small on the fixed 300-item panel.

A degradation alert is registered when all of the following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $b>c$;
    \item $p_{\mathrm{exact}}<\alpha=0.05$;
    \item the observed accuracy drop satisfies
    \[
        \hat{A}_{t_1}-\hat{A}_{t_2}
        =
        \frac{b-c}{n}
        \ge \Delta_{\min},
    \]
    with $\Delta_{\min}=0.05$.
\end{enumerate}

Conditions (i) and (ii) provide statistical evidence of net degradation on the fixed panel. Condition (iii) adds a practical-significance requirement aligned with the design target from Chapter~\ref{ch:data}. Without such a condition, very small absolute drops can become statistically significant when discordant pairs are sufficiently imbalanced.

If $b+c=0$, there are no discordant pairs and no directional conclusion is drawn.

Because the monitoring rule is applied repeatedly over time, $\alpha=0.05$ should be interpreted as a \emph{per-comparison} threshold rather than as a long-run false-alert guarantee. Under repeated monitoring, cumulative false-alert probability increases over time.

\subsection{Empirical examples from monitoring runs}

Tables~\ref{tab:mcnemar_gpt5_meth} and~\ref{tab:mcnemar_gpt5mini_meth} show paired contingency tables from two batch comparisons on the $n=300$ monitoring subset (8~March~2026 vs.\ 10~March~2026).

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{McNemar table for \texttt{gpt-5} between 8~March~2026 and 10~March~2026 on $\mathcal{D}_n$.}
    \label{tab:mcnemar_gpt5_meth}
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        & 2026-03-10 correct & 2026-03-10 wrong \\
        \hline
        2026-03-08 correct & 291 & 0 \\
        2026-03-08 wrong   & 0   & 9 \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Here $(b,c)=(0,0)$, so there are no discordant pairs. Operationally, no degradation alert is issued and no directional change is detected on the fixed panel.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{McNemar table for \texttt{gpt-5.1-mini} between 8~March~2026 and 10~March~2026 on $\mathcal{D}_n$.}
    \label{tab:mcnemar_gpt5mini_meth}
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        & 2026-03-10 correct & 2026-03-10 wrong \\
        \hline
        2026-03-08 correct & 269 & 13 \\
        2026-03-08 wrong   & 7   & 11 \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Here $(b,c)=(13,7)$, so $n_{\mathrm{dis}}=20$ and
\[
    p_{\mathrm{exact}}
    =
    \sum_{k=13}^{20}\binom{20}{k}2^{-20}
    \approx 0.132.
\]
As a large-sample summary,
\[
    \chi^2_{\mathrm{cc}}
    =
    \frac{(|13-7|-1)^2}{20}
    =
    \frac{25}{20}
    =
    1.25.
\]
The observed accuracy change is
\[
    \hat{A}_{t_2}-\hat{A}_{t_1}
    =
    \frac{c-b}{n}
    =
    \frac{7-13}{300}
    =
    -0.02.
\]
Thus the panel accuracy dropped by $2$ percentage points. Although $b>c$, the one-sided exact evidence does not meet $\alpha=0.05$, and the observed drop is also below the practical threshold $\Delta_{\min}=0.05$. Therefore no degradation alert is triggered under the operational rule above.

\section{Classification metrics on the monitoring subset}
\label{sec:classification-metrics}

All metrics below are computed on $\mathcal{D}_n$ (Section~\ref{sec:informative-sampling-disagreement}). They describe behaviour on the disagreement-enriched panel; their levels and rankings need not match the corresponding full-benchmark metrics on $\mathcal{D}_N$ (Section~\ref{sec:selection-bias}).

Let $\mathrm{TP}_t$, $\mathrm{FP}_t$, $\mathrm{TN}_t$, and $\mathrm{FN}_t$ be confusion counts on $\mathcal{D}_n$ at time $t$ for a given model and a fixed rule mapping provider output to $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$. Omit any ratio whose denominator is zero.

\paragraph{Accuracy.}
Empirical accuracy is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{TN}_t}
         {\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{TN}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t+\mathrm{FN}_t}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed model and parsing rule, this equals $\hat{A}_t$. It summarises global correctness but weights errors by class prevalence on $\mathcal{D}_n$.

\paragraph{Precision, recall, and $F_1$.}
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t}{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TP}_t}{\mathrm{TP}_t+\mathrm{FN}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{F}}_{1,t}
    =
    \frac{2\,\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t\,\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t}
         {\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t}.
\end{equation}
Precision is positive predictive value; recall is sensitivity on the positive class. The $F_1$ score is their harmonic mean when both are defined and $\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t>0$.

\paragraph{Specificity and balanced accuracy.}
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{Spec}}_t
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{TN}_t}{\mathrm{TN}_t+\mathrm{FP}_t},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathrm{BA}}_t
    =
    \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t+\widehat{\mathrm{Spec}}_t\bigr).
\end{equation}
Specificity measures true-negative performance. Balanced accuracy mitigates inflation of raw accuracy under prevalence skew on the panel.

\paragraph{Hard labels only.}
The monitoring pipeline maps provider outputs to a single hard label $\hat{y}_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$ via a fixed parsing rule; no per-example graded score is logged for the experiments reported here. The metrics above are therefore computed from one confusion table per model and batch. Threshold-sweep summaries such as precision--recall curves or PR--AUC are not identifiable from hard-label outputs alone and are omitted. Degradation alerts remain based on McNemar's test on hard correctness (Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section}).

\paragraph{Which metric is most diagnostic on the monitoring panel.}
On the disagreement-enriched panel, prevalence is high (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}), so raw accuracy can hide asymmetric errors. Recall should dominate when missed relevant posts are expensive (high FN burden), while specificity should dominate when over-flagging irrelevant posts is operationally costly (high FP burden). Precision is useful when analyst workload is constrained by false alarms. Balanced accuracy can be more informative than raw accuracy when class imbalance is strong. McNemar remains the inferential test for panel-level degradation alerts.

A limitation is that McNemar on total correctness may not fully reveal class-specific shifts when improvements in one error type are offset by regressions in another. For that reason, confusion-derived metrics are reported alongside the inferential alert rule.

\section{Model comparison and deployment choice}
\label{sec:model-comparison-manual}

Candidate models $m\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cand}}$ may first be screened on $\mathcal{D}_n$ using $\hat{A}_t(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{Prec}}_t(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{Rec}}_t(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{F}}_{1,t}(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{Spec}}_t(m)$, $\widehat{\mathrm{BA}}_t(m)$, and observed mean inference cost $C_t(m)$ to obtain a quick descriptive profile on the drift-sensitive panel.

However, because $\mathcal{D}_n$ is disagreement-enriched, strongly class-skewed, and visually heavier than the full benchmark, these subset-level quantities are not by themselves an appropriate basis for final deployment-cost estimation or constrained deployment selection. In particular, subset-level mean inference cost is descriptive for the monitored panel only and need not equal deployment-representative average cost. Final deployment choice under Chapter~\ref{ch:cost-deployment-utility} should therefore be made on the full benchmark $\mathcal{D}_N$ or another representative evaluation set.

Two decision layers are kept separate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item inferential detection of degradation for $m_{\mathrm{curr}}$ on the fixed monitoring panel $\mathcal{D}_n$ via the exact one-sided McNemar rule in Section~\ref{sec:mcnemar-section};
    \item if action is required, full-benchmark comparison of candidate models using the cost-aware or constrained rules from Chapter~\ref{ch:cost-deployment-utility}.
\end{enumerate}

Campaigns differ in the relative cost of false positives and false negatives. While Chapter~\ref{ch:cost-deployment-utility} presents a cost-based utility as one formalisation of the accuracy--cost trade-off, final deployment decisions incorporate manual judgment alongside these quantitative criteria, including tolerance to error types, budget, latency, and provider policy. If no acceptable fallback exists, prompt optimisation may be attempted before changing the API model (Chapter~\ref{chap:prompt-optimisation}). Exported numeric panels and dashboards used in experiments are described in Chapter~\ref{chap:system-architecture}.
